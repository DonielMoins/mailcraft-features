| 2026-11-12 | M7: spelling and grammar while writing (#954). A dictionary's words are read from this computer (Hunspell `.dic`/`.aff`: stems with their flags, prefixes and suffixes with their conditions and cross-products, `NEEDAFFIX`, `FORBIDDENWORD`, `NOSUGGEST`) and every dictionary that is on is consulted for every word, so a word is right when any of them knows it — English and French in one paragraph need no switching, and nothing is flagged for the mixing itself. Spelling places are found in the body, suggestions are ranked by the edits it takes with the same first letter preferred, and the owner's own words are kept in the settings (never in the system's files). A correction is one step of the message's own undo and keeps the look of what it replaced, and a place that no longer fits the text is refused rather than applied to other words. Grammar is a LanguageTool server's, `POST /v2/check`, one request at a time with the text resting between them: only this computer's, unless the owner allows another, and refused by the window, the engine and the network layer each on its own account. Offsets come back in UTF-16 units and are converted to bytes once, with tests for accents and emoji; a hostile or oversized answer is dropped, never clamped. The window draws a red line under what no dictionary knows and a blue one under what the server found, right click offers what could stand there instead, and Review › Proofing › Spelling & Grammar walks through them; File › Options › Spelling & Grammar has the switches, the dictionaries, the owner's words and the server |
